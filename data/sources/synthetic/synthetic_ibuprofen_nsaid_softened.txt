Ibuprofen for adults (Nurofen)

Other common brands: Brufen, Calprofen, Fenbid, Ibugel, Ibuleve

Ibuprofen is a painkiller that helps relieve pain and reduce swelling (inflammation). You can buy most types from shops and pharmacies, but some types are only available with a prescription.

This information is for adults aged 18 or over.

What ibuprofen is for

Ibuprofen is a type of painkiller called a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID).

Ibuprofen and other NSAIDs can help with lots of types of pain, including headaches and migraines, joint and muscle pain, pain after an injury, toothache, and period pain.

How to take ibuprofen and how much to take (dose)

Always check the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine. Ask a pharmacist or doctor for advice if you're not sure how to use it or have any problems using it.

Important

To reduce the risk of side effects, take the smallest dose you need for the shortest time you need it.

Do not take ibuprofen tablets and capsules for more than 10 days unless advised to by a doctor.

See a GP if your pain gets worse or does not go away.

Side effects of ibuprofen

Common side effects of ibuprofen tablets, capsules, liquid and granules include indigestion and stomach aches, feeling sick (nausea) and being sick (vomiting), headaches, a rash, dizziness, diarrhoea or constipation.

Serious side effects

Possible serious side effects of ibuprofen include stomach ulcers or bleeding inside your stomach, an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis), and problems with your kidneys.

The risk of serious side effects is higher if you're taking a high dose of ibuprofen or taking it for a long time.

Who can and cannot use ibuprofen

Most adults can use ibuprofen. But ibuprofen is not suitable for everyone. For example, it may not be suitable if you've had an allergic reaction to ibuprofen, aspirin or another NSAID before, or you have a stomach ulcer or you've had them before.

Taking ibuprofen with other medicines, food and drink

There are some medicines, remedies and supplements that may not mix well with ibuprofen, including some types of anticoagulants (such as warfarin), steroid medicines, antidepressants, and medicines to lower blood pressure.

Taking ibuprofen with other painkillers

Adults can take ibuprofen at the same time as paracetamol if needed, but it's a good idea to try them on their own first to see if they relieve your pain.

Ibuprofen is one of several NSAIDs, alongside naproxen and aspirin, that people sometimes combine for stronger pain relief — it's worth checking with a pharmacist about your particular combination.

Food, drink and alcohol with ibuprofen

You can eat and drink normally while taking ibuprofen, but try to avoid drinking a lot of alcohol because this can increase the risk of side effects.
