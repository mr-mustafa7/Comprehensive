Paracetamol for adults

Reviewed by J. Okoye, Medicines Information Pharmacist (GPhC 2118456) · 17 Oct 2026

How long to take it for

Do not take paracetamol for more than 3 days unless a doctor tells you to. Taking it for longer raises the risk of liver damage.

✓ Corrected from an earlier AI draft that read "shouldn't be stopped before 3 days are up, unless your doctor hasn't said otherwise" — flagged by the comprehension-gap checker as a reversed-instruction risk before this reached a patient.

Same draft, two timelines

Without the checker

Draft
AI drafts the leaflet, including the confusing sentence.
Publish
Leaflet is approved on a readability pass — the sentence reads at a normal grade level, so nothing looks wrong.
Patient reads it
Patient reads "shouldn't be stopped before 3 days are up" and understands it as a 3-day minimum.
Outcome
Patient keeps taking paracetamol past the point the leaflet itself warns against — the exact prolonged-use risk the real NHS guidance names.

With the checker

Draft
AI drafts the same leaflet, including the same sentence.
Flagged
Judge compares a simulated layperson's teach-back against the source and flags reversed_instruction, confidence 0.95.
Reviewed
Pharmacist opens the queued flag, agrees, rewrites the sentence before publication.
Outcome
Patient reads the corrected instruction, understands the 3-day maximum, stops safely.

What this is and isn't: both timelines dramatize a single evaluated test case from this project's own n=8 labelled set (synthetic_paracetamol_duration_reversed — a real NHS paracetamol page with one sentence we deliberately rewrote, clearly labelled synthetic, to test exactly this failure mode). The checker caught it at 0.95 confidence in our evaluation. This page illustrates the mechanism and does not claim this outcome has occurred to a real patient, or estimate how often it would. No real patient, prescriber, or NHS system is represented here.
